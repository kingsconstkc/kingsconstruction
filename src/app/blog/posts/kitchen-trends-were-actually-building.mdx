## What we're seeing on real jobs

We spend our days in KC kitchens — demo, installs, punch lists — so we get an honest read on what homeowners actually want versus what Pinterest says they should want. Here's the current mix.

## Warm dark tones are back

Navy and charcoal cabinetry with brass and gold hardware — the exact palette behind our own logo — are showing up in more of our projects. The trick that makes it feel residential instead of restaurant-dark: warm wood on the island or open shelving, and layered lighting instead of one overhead fixture.

## The kitchen is the living room now

Open floor plans stopped being a trend and became the default. That means the kitchen has to look as good as it works: integrated appliances, appliance garages for the counter clutter, and backsplashes treated like wall art. It also means the plumbing and electrical rough-in has to serve a room with no walls hiding it.

## But the workhorse choices win

What homeowners thank us for a year later isn't a trend at all:

- **Quartz over marble** for the main prep zone — it survives red wine and kids
- **A real pantry**, even when it costs cabinet run elsewhere
- **Two dishwashers' worth of plumbing roughed in** if the layout allows — even if one gets capped
- **Scrubbable paint** on every wall within kid reach

## What we'd skip

All-black everything (shows every fingerprint), white grout in high-spray zones, and any fixture that requires a ladder to clean. If a trend adds a maintenance chore, it's renting your kitchen, not improving it.
