## Why this list exists

We've been on enough job sites to know that a bad remodel rarely starts with bad work. It starts with a vague quote, an unclear timeline, and a homeowner who didn't know what to ask. So here's the list we'd hand our own neighbors — including the questions that are fair to ask *us*.

## 1. "Are you licensed, bonded, and insured — and can I see it?"

Any contractor should produce proof of general liability insurance and workers' comp without hesitation. If it takes three phone calls and a follow-up email, that's your answer about how the project will go.

## 2. "Who is actually going to be in my house?"

The person who sells the job is often not the person who runs it. Ask who the on-site lead is, how many crews will touch the project, and how subcontractors are vetted. A straightforward answer is a sign of a straightforward job.

## 3. "What's the change-order process?"

Every remodel has surprises once walls open up. The question isn't *if* — it's how you'll be told and approved. Good process: written change order, price and schedule impact, your sign-off before work proceeds. If "we'll figure it out" is the whole answer, keep shopping.

## 4. "What does the timeline look like — honestly?"

Ask for the sequence, not just an end date: demo, rough-in, inspection, finishes. Then ask what typically pushes it — material lead times, inspection scheduling, weather on exterior work. A contractor who's done your kind of project can name the usual snags.

## 5. "What happens after we finish?"

Warranty on workmanship, what's covered, how punch-list items get handled. We stand behind our work — and we'd expect any contractor you hire to say the same in writing.

## The bottom line

A remodel is a trust business. The right contractor won't dodge a single question on this list — they'll respect that you asked.
