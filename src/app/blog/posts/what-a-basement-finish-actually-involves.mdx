## The room nobody sees

A finished basement is 20% finish work and 80% decisions made before any drywall goes up. When a quote looks suspiciously low, it's almost always because those invisible steps got dropped. Here's the real sequence.

## 1. Moisture first, always

Before framing, we test for moisture: tape plastic to the walls and floor, wait, and read what condenses. Then the walls get sealed, a proper vapor strategy goes in, and we check the exterior grading and downspouts while we're at it. Framing straight over a damp wall is how you build a mold problem with nice recessed lighting.

## 2. Rough-in while it's open

Electrical, data, plumbing for a wet bar or bathroom, and HVAC zoning all get run while the walls are open. This is also the moment to plan head height: framing and ductwork layout determines whether the finished ceiling clears code — or feels like a cave.

## 3. Egress and safety, not paperwork

Bedroom-level finishes require an egress window of the right size in the right place, and smoke/CO devices go in on a hardwired loop. We treat these as design constraints, not inspection chores — a basement that fails egress is a basement you can't sell bedrooms in.

## 4. Then the fun part

Only now does it become a room: framing to the ceiling grid for maximum height, sound isolation between the mechanical closet and the living space, the media wall layout, the bar plumbing set to the cabinetry plan. Moisture-tolerant flooring throughout — basements breathe, and materials should too.

## Why the order matters

Every shortcut in steps 1–3 shows up in years two through five: musty air, buckled flooring, an inspector who red-flags the whole permit. Every contractor quotes step 4. The difference is whether they do steps 1–3 honestly.
